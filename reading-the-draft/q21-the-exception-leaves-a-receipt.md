# The exception that leaves a receipt: VOC and the readable refusal | Aicent Stack

30 Oct 2026 · [Read the full article](https://blog.aicent.com/q21-the-exception-leaves-a-receipt/) · [Chinese version](https://blog.aicent.com/zh/q21-the-exception-leaves-a-receipt/)

The thread moved from correction to design. In two posts on one evening, the counterpart who has driven this series since No. 15 laid out the delegation-policy landscape as it actually exists, and then reached for the piece every delegation system is missing - what happens when the delegator needs an exception. Reading the Draft, No. 21: the experiment he named Voluntary Oblivious Compliance, where a hard block taught people to route around and a readable block taught them to explain; the two options a delegator could declare; where those options must live (a caveat on the delegation, one layer up, never a field on the wire); and what the wire adds instead - the receipt side. Which option was declared, the reason given, the state the decision was computed over - read by the delegator when the delegator chooses to look, not pushed by the verifier. The options say what the delegator wanted; the receipt says what actually happened, and on what evidence.
