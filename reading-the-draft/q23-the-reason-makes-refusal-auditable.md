# The reason is what makes a refusal auditable — three refusal vocabularies met on the walls this fortnight, and ours came with vectors | Aicent Stack

3 Nov 2026 · [Read the full article](https://blog.aicent.com/q23-the-reason-makes-refusal-auditable/) · [Chinese version](https://blog.aicent.com/zh/q23-the-reason-makes-refusal-auditable/)

This fortnight the IETF WIMSE list watched a third closed refusal vocabulary take shape in public — a reviewer objected that one status code folded six distinct situations, the split followed, and the thread's opener closed with "Preserving the reason matters for subsequent review and audit." — while a second thread fought over whether a missing proof and a proof never issued are distinguishable, and the W3C wall heard the credential-sharing argument stated in one sentence again. Reading the Draft series, No. 23: the wall record, and the vector family we built so our own vocabulary would not have to win arguments — aicent-grant-ref, twenty-five cases that pin the five-value refusal vocabulary, its priority order, the null rule for the observation member, and the five-member grant reference to the published text, with a mutation battery that proves the vectors can tell a correct implementation from a broken one.
